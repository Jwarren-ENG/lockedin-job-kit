# Prepare for an interview

Read the application record, submitted resume, recruiter instructions and confirmed interview date. Identify which stage is actually confirmed. Ask for the date or missing format when it changes the plan; do independent research meanwhile.

Research official role/product materials first. When the user requests Reddit or other candidate accounts, search recent role/team-specific reports. Separate completed firsthand recaps from questions, speculation, promotional replies and other job tracks. Track post dates versus follow-up dates and deduplicate cross-posts. Do not invent a universal sequence, passing probability or guaranteed questions.

Create a practical dated plan in interviews/<company-role>/ with 30-minute fallback and normal study time, concrete daily deliverables, truthful experience stories, relevant technical exercises, a mock interview and final logistics. Keep the current stage the priority. Build a skills-to-learn list tied to actual role needs. Distinguish reported questions from original practice exercises. Never invent user stories or metrics.

Use public or synthetic data. Rehearse the exact resume claims and what the person did versus teammates. For a mock, ask one question at a time and probe reasoning, then give specific feedback. Record scheduling as user-reported unless independently verified. Do not schedule reminders unless requested; reuse an existing job-search schedule if supported and authorized.
