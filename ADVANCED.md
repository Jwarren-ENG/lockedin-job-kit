# Set up an ongoing search with #LOCKEDIN Job Kit

Choose Advanced if you want an assistant to maintain application files and revisit employers between sessions. Larger hosted usage budgets can accommodate this work. The first test below shows what your particular assistant can actually do. You can start in a normal chat and add tools later.

## Get the files ready

1. Unzip the Advanced download. Make a second, private folder called **My Job Search** for your answers, résumés and tracker. The kit folder is the clean copy you can share.
2. In Codex, open the kit as a project. In Claude Desktop with Cowork, select the kit and grant access to the private folder when prompted. In a chat with file uploads, attach the starter or paste its text and save the results yourself. The [app instructions](START_HERE.md) cover each route.
3. Open **editions/ADVANCED_STARTER.txt** and send its contents. Attach your résumé or paste your work history. You can start without a posting. If you prefer filling in a form, open **assets/setup.html** in your browser. It works offline. Save its downloaded answers in **My Job Search**, then attach them to the private setup conversation.
4. Tell the assistant “Read the start instructions, interview me about the missing details and show me what you learned.” It should first read your résumé, list the facts it found and ask you to confirm them. After that, it offers the history choice below and asks for missing application details. It can initialize the tracker once you authorize the folder. CSV works with common spreadsheet apps. The assistant should open the resulting file and show its location.

## Choose whether to include earlier chats

The default is to use your confirmed résumé and interview answers. You can paste a few lines from another conversation, or give permission to read specific conversations for this setup. For example, “Use only my inventory project retrospective in this project.” The assistant should say what it can actually open. Pick individual conversations using your app’s controls where available, or paste just the relevant excerpt. You can change your mind at any point.

For each possible addition, the assistant will identify where it came from and ask whether to **use**, **edit** or **leave out** that detail. Old plans and team achievements need your confirmation before they describe your own work. Keep the approved fact and a short private source reference. The routine job search schedule has no reason to revisit these chats.

## Build a résumé you can defend

Start with the work you want to do, your level, locations, remote eligibility and when you can start. Give a hard minimum and a preferred base salary separately. Explain whether a range that reaches your minimum is worth reviewing. Include travel and relocation limits. If your target is unclear, describe a day you enjoyed at work and one you would avoid.

Pick one experience that looks unremarkable on your résumé. Who relied on the work? What surprised you? What decision did you make yourself? How did you know it worked? If the result was a decision or a process others adopted, say so. A percentage needs a defensible baseline. Try the same questions for paid work, school, caregiving, a club, volunteering or something you built independently. Keep confidential details out. Review the wording and your share of any team result.

For example, “helped with scheduling” could mean “Built a shift calendar that let weekend volunteers see unfilled shifts before Friday.” That version belongs on the résumé only if the person built it and volunteers used it that way. Save the original title and dates alongside the confirmed story. Each submission should retain its own dated copy of the exact résumé and material answers.

## Choose where to look

Ask for a shortlist of sources with a reason to use each. For an associate product search, the assistant might try [APM List](https://apmlist.com/), the relevant category in [Simplify’s new graduate list](https://github.com/SimplifyJobs/New-Grad-Positions) and employers hiring for that graduation year. A purchasing specialist might start with manufacturers in commuting range and a supply management association. A nurse might start with regional hospital systems and postings that specify the relevant license.

Open a sample listing on the employer site. Check the actual duties, location, pay basis, start date and eligibility against your criteria. Keep useful sources in your private source list. Tell the assistant what it missed. An aggregator repost, a stale cohort or a page the assistant could not open should be labeled accordingly.

## Give the assistant the access you want

Start by choosing the job: research only, prepare drafts, or submit matching applications within a daily cap. Then select the individual tools it needs. Your saved application answers and the app’s own device permissions are separate.

| Choice | What to set up and test |
| --- | --- |
| Private files | Select **My Job Search** in the app’s folder picker. Ask it to save and reopen a sample tracker row there. |
| Browser and forms | Enable the browser or computer tool in your assistant if available. Choose the relevant sites or tab when prompted. If your app offers a remembered choice, use it only for the scope you want. First test by reading a public posting. |
| Contact and address | Choose the email and phone employers may receive. Confirm current address, permanent address and preferred work location separately. Decide which address may be shared, with which recipients and for what question. The form can save a full address privately without authorizing disclosure. |
| Employer accounts | If wanted, connect your password manager through its normal autofill flow. Have it generate and save a unique password for each employer portal. Keep passwords out of chats, tracker files and résumés. |
| Recruiting consents | Record ordinary applicant data processing separately from talent pools, sharing with partners, background checks, AI recording and optional demographic disclosures. Save what you picked, when, and whether it applies to one employer or to matching future forms. |
| Job email | Select the intended account and authorize job related reads if desired. Check one known application message. Email monitoring does not authorize replies. |
| Submission | Review the first draft and its attachments. Choose your daily cap and which roles or employers are in scope. A portal can require a fresh signature, login or user action even if standing preferences are saved. |

Ask the assistant to read back the choices and list any missing information together. A confirmed reusable answer can be used again in scope. A new employer asking about its own conflict of interest needs facts about that employer. To change a choice, name it directly, for example “Stop sharing my permanent address. Keep preparing drafts.”

## Schedule your search

1. Pick days, time, time zone, search mode and maximum attempts. Choose how often to check job email, if enabled. A single daily email check usually suffices even if searches run more often. Decide which changes deserve a notification.
2. Ask: “Check for an existing job search schedule. Update that schedule or create one for weekdays at 8 AM in my time zone. Search my chosen sources, prepare up to two drafts and notify me about new matches or replies.” Substitute your actual time, mode and cap.
3. In an assistant with scheduling tools, have it save the task in the host and read it back. Confirm the task name, active status, next local run and which folder and tools the scheduled session can reach. Keep the scheduler’s identifier privately so the same task can be updated later. A draft prompt alone has no next run.
4. Run a discovery or draft test with submissions off. Check the tracker and run history. For persistent monitoring, verify the first scheduled execution in the host history. If the scheduler requires an open app or an awake computer, keep it available during that time. A host that only offers reminders can remind you to open the kit. A chat with no scheduler can give you reminder text to add yourself.
5. To stop, say “Pause my job search and its scheduled task.” Check that the native task is paused and the tracker blocks future submissions. To restart, specify the desired permissions and schedule once.

## Use your time and message budget

Open the [workload planner](docs/index.html) before changing a scheduled batch size. Adjust the Advanced assumptions to include research, tailoring and review. The calculator changes only the estimate. Change a live schedule or application cap separately.

Start a run by checking the tracker for duplicates and recent source coverage. Rank leads in a short table before spending messages on tailored résumés. Review new or changed requirements once, group outstanding factual questions, and save a short end-of-run summary. Multiple workers need one shared reservation per employer and requisition, one daily limit, and a shared record of receipts. If the host cannot coordinate those writes, run one application worker. There is no reason to increase a daily cap because a higher subscription is available.

## Check your first result

Ask to see one verified match, the résumé edit that makes your experience clearer, the role’s outstanding question and its tracker row. Open the exact saved résumé. A submitted application needs an employer receipt or your explicit confirmation. An interrupted submission stays uncertain while the assistant checks the portal. An interview invitation becomes a scheduled interview once you have a date and time.

Later, ask “Which résumés did I send for the roles that invited me to interview?” This is why each row retains the version actually used. Review patterns across comparable roles before changing your approach. The [optional Obsidian guide](OPTIONAL_OBSIDIAN.md) adds an interview dashboard whenever you want one.
